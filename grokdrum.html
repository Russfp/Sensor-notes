Kick Drum

The low “boom”. It usually hits on beats 1 and 3 (or every beat in electronic music). Provides the foundation and pulse.

Snare

The sharp “crack”. Most often played on beats 2 and 4. Creates the backbeat that makes you want to clap or nod.

Hi-Hat

The ticking “tss”. Usually plays 8th or 16th notes to keep time. Can be open or closed (we’re using a closed sound here).

Clap

Handclap-style sound. Often replaces or doubles the snare. Great for adding energy and human feel.

16-Step Sequencer

Each row is one sound. Each column is a 16th-note step. A full bar of 4/4 time has 16 steps. Darker vertical lines mark the quarter-note beats.

How to Practice

1. Load a pattern
2. Listen and watch the lights
3. Mute tracks by clearing them
4. Change BPM and feel the difference
5. Build your own after the examples
